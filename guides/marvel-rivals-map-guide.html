Marvel Rivals map guide: make the objective decide the lineup

A map decision starts before hero select. Identify what must be contested, how the team enters that area, which teammates need protection and where everyone regroups when the first attempt fails.

Evidence boundary: a map-specific hero claim needs a dated source and applicable version, mode and route context. This guide therefore teaches repeatable map planning instead of inventing “best hero on every map” tables.

The four map questions before hero select

1. What is the objective?

State the immediate win condition: contesting an area, holding space, reaching the next safe position or preventing an opponent’s advance. The objective decides which team job matters first.

2. Where is the entry?

Name one main entry route and one safer alternative. Then decide who creates space, who follows, and who stays able to protect a vulnerable teammate.

3. Which sightline is dangerous?

Do not call a hero weak because a single lane is hard. Ask whether the team lacks cover timing, pressure on the lane, protection for an ally or a clearer route.

4. Where is the reset?

Agree on the condition for leaving a lost fight and the regroup route. A repeatable reset protects time and prevents isolated players from creating a second loss.

Map-to-composition decision sheet

  1. Objective job: name who can contest or preserve the important space.
  2. Entry job: name who starts pressure and who follows that timing.
  3. Protection job: name who keeps exposed teammates connected to the plan.
  4. Finish job: name how an opening becomes a confirmed advantage rather than a chase.
  5. Reset job: name the call that ends a lost attempt and protects the next one.

These jobs complement the role labels on the hero pages. Use Team Composer to see whether the selected lineup has framework gaps, then verify version-dependent details on the relevant official source.

Between-fight review

  • Did the team reach the intended entry together?
  • Did an ally lose protection before the objective was contested?
  • Was the pressure target clear enough to create a finish?
  • Did the team leave a lost fight together?
  • What is the one route, timing or coverage change to test next?

Should you use a Marvel Rivals map tier list?

A tier list can be a useful prompt only when its patch, mode, platform, rank range, sample, collection period and methodology are visible. It cannot replace the current objective and your team’s execution. Treat an undated list or clip as a hypothesis, then test one specific map decision.

How map pages will be updated

When a map, mode or route detail has a dated official source, this guide can link a focused page with its source date, version and revision history. The first example is Thebes: official launch details and a first-match plan. Until a source supports more, no map-specific hero advantage is presented as fact. This keeps seasonal content useful without turning old advice into a false current ranking.
